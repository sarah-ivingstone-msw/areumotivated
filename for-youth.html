The Connection Hour for Young People

Help young people build practical skills before the pressures of adulthood make them harder to develop.

Why It Matters

Building Skills Early

Young people face real pressures — at school, at home, in relationships, and increasingly online. Academic demands, social dynamics, and the transition toward adulthood create conditions where stress, miscommunication, and unhealthy patterns can easily take hold.

The Connection Hour gives young people a space to talk through real-life situations and practice skills they can actually use — not in a clinical setting, but in an honest, guided conversation facilitated by Sarah Livingstone, MSW.

The practical skills developed through these sessions can support young people as they navigate the increasing demands of school, relationships, and everyday life — and build a foundation that carries into adulthood.

Building communication, coping, and life skills early — before unhealthy patterns take hold.

  • Discussion-based, not lecture-based
  • Appropriate for grades 6–12
  • Sessions for teens only, guardians only, or jointly
  • In-person or virtual delivery
  • Facilitated with care for developmental stage
  • Not therapy — practical skill-building
What Young People Practice

Practical Skills for Real Life

Topics are adapted to fit the age, needs, and context of the young people in the room. The conversation is always guided in a way that is respectful, relevant, and developmentally appropriate.

Communication

Expressing thoughts and feelings clearly, listening with intention, and navigating difficult conversations without shutting down or escalating.

Coping with Stress

Recognizing stress as it builds and developing practical tools for managing it before it becomes overwhelming.

Boundaries

Understanding personal limits, learning to communicate them, and recognizing when boundaries are being pushed or crossed.

Conflict

Moving from reaction to response — practical skills for handling disagreement in friendships, family relationships, and school settings.

Resilience

Building the ability to recover, adapt, and keep going — even when things feel hard or uncertain.

Emotional Awareness

Learning to notice and name emotions, and understanding how emotions shape choices and relationships.

Healthy Habits

Identifying everyday habits — sleep, self-care, routines — that support mental and emotional well-being over time.

Relationships

Exploring what healthy relationships look like and practicing the skills that help build and maintain them.

Managing Change

Navigating transitions — new schools, new situations, growing up — with steadiness and practical tools.

How Sessions Work

Flexible Formats for Youth & Families

The Connection Hour can be shaped around the needs of your organization, school, or program.

For Organizations & Schools

  • Youth-only sessions for students
  • Guardian-only sessions for parents and caregivers
  • Joint sessions for teens and guardians together
  • Pilot program: 3 Saturday sessions, 1 hour each
  • Available for grades 6–12
  • In-person or virtual

For Families

  • Private family sessions available
  • Parent/guardian sessions to strengthen support skills
  • Teen-focused sessions with parent context
  • Discussion-based — not therapy or counseling
  • In-person or virtual
  • Custom format based on your family's needs
