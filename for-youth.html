The Connection Hour for Young People

Young people have an opportunity to build practical skills early — before the demands of school, relationships, and adulthood increase.

Why It Matters

Building Skills Early

Young people face real pressures — at school, at home, in relationships, and increasingly online. The demands of adolescence and the transition toward adulthood create conditions where stress, miscommunication, and unhealthy patterns can take hold before young people have the skills to navigate them.

The Connection Hour gives young people a space to talk through real-life situations and practice skills they can actually use — in an honest, guided conversation facilitated by Sarah Livingstone, MSW.

This is not therapy. It is practical, discussion-based skill-building — and it meets young people where they are.

Building communication, coping, and life skills early — before unhealthy patterns become harder to change.

  • Discussion-based, not lecture-based
  • Appropriate for grades 6–12
  • Sessions for teens only, guardians only, or jointly
  • In-person or virtual delivery
  • Facilitated with care for developmental stage
  • Not therapy — practical skill-building
What Young People Practice

Practical Skills for Real Life

Topics are drawn from the full Connection Hour curriculum and adapted to fit the age, needs, and context of the young people in the room. The conversation is always guided in a way that is respectful, relevant, and developmentally appropriate.

Conflict Resolution

Practical skills for handling disagreement in friendships, family relationships, and school settings — without shutting down or escalating.

Reaction vs. Response

Practicing the pause between feeling something and acting on it — a foundational skill for healthier communication.

Dealing with Stress

Recognizing stress as it builds and developing practical tools for managing it before it becomes overwhelming.

Managing Change

Navigating transitions — new schools, new situations, growing up — with steadiness and practical tools.

Boundaries / Survival / Growth

Understanding personal limits, learning to communicate them, and recognizing when boundaries are being pushed or crossed.

Accepting Your Truth

Developing honest self-awareness and the ability to acknowledge personal patterns, needs, and areas for growth.

Resilience & Protection

Building the ability to recover, adapt, and keep going — even when things feel hard or uncertain.

The Power of Habits

Identifying everyday habits — sleep, self-care, routines — that support mental and emotional well-being over time.

The Impact of Bullying

Understanding the dynamics and effects of bullying on individuals, relationships, and communities — and building practical responses.

Concern & Empathy

Building the capacity to care about others' experiences and communicate that care in meaningful ways.

Avoiding Self-Sabotage

Recognizing the patterns that get in the way of growth, relationships, and goals — and building healthier alternatives.

Self-Care & Affirmations

Practical self-care strategies and the role of affirming self-talk in managing stress and supporting well-being.

Managing Needs & Resources

Understanding and balancing personal needs with available resources — and developing practical strategies for everyday life.

How Sessions Work

Flexible Formats for Youth & Families

The Connection Hour can be shaped around the needs of your organization, school, or program.

For Organizations & Schools

  • Youth-only sessions for students
  • Guardian-only sessions for parents and caregivers
  • Joint sessions for teens and guardians together
  • Begin with a 3-Session Pilot Experience
  • Available for grades 6–12
  • In-person or virtual

For Families

  • Private family sessions available
  • Parent/guardian sessions to strengthen support skills
  • Teen-focused sessions with parent context
  • Discussion-based — not therapy or counseling
  • In-person or virtual
  • Custom format based on your family's needs
